Terms. A task run is one task given to the model once for one condition and seed, with at most one retry. There are 630 of them, and the model was called 1,115 times counting retries. First try (pass@1) is the first answer of a task run. When a table says “out of 60”, it means 60 task runs.

The primary metric was fixed before the run: pass@1, meaning the very first answer passes every hidden test, on the similar and transfer tasks. That is 18 tasks, each tried on 5 seeds, so 90 attempts per condition. A retry is allowed afterwards, with the failing test's message, but it does not count for pass@1.

Worth noticing. In the experience phase Wrong notes solved 7 of 60 on the first try, while No memory solved 0. In the evaluation phases it solved 0 of 90. So irrelevant notes can leak a little useful information while memory is small and every lesson is short, which is a reason the primary metric is measured on frozen memory and why the control is reported, not hidden.

Memory worked best where the lesson is a single, unambiguous example: dates and error messages were solved first try on all 15 attempts by Lucifer. It was weakest on identifiers (3 of 15). The identifier tasks shipment_id and ticket_id were never solved first try (0 of 5 and 0 of 5 seeds), although all five seeds passed them after the retry. The earlier lessons show ORD-, INV- and CUS-, so a model has to infer “first three letters of the kind”, and SHP or TKT are natural-looking alternatives. That is a reading of the numbers, not a tested cause; it is listed on the Limits page as a task-design question.

Look also at the “No memory · after retry” column. Error messages are hard even with feedback (33% after one retry), which is where Lucifer's lessons help most.

Transfer is the weak spot: Lucifer solved 5 of 30 first try (17%). Five of the six transfer tasks needed two or three conventions at once, or a prefix not seen in any lesson. Four of those were never solved first try and one was solved on 1 of 5 seeds. The one that needed a single convention (total_line, prices) was solved on 4 of 5 seeds. After one retry Lucifer reaches 53% on transfer, against 13% for No memory.

How to read this. The conventions are hidden, so No memory was not expected to pass first try, and did not (0 of 210 task runs in the whole run). But it can fix the mistake once it sees the failing test: 52% pass after one retry. What memory buys is skipping the failed first try. After a retry the gap is about 30 points (82% against 52%), not 50.

Wrong notes cost the most tokens (754 per task), almost twice Lucifer, and bought nothing on the primary metric. Lucifer wrote shorter answers and needed fewer retries, so it was also the fastest on the runner (2.5 hours of model time across its 210 tasks, against 3.8 for No memory). These are 4-CPU runner timings with a 14B model; they say little about a GPU.

Gate 2 · Retention

On tasks the baseline reliably solves (at least 80%, chosen on half the seeds), Lucifer may lose at most 5 points on the other half.
